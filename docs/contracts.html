Two things are not fork-safe: - **The heap.** A fork while another thread holds one of the heap's locks leaves the child stuck at its first allocation that takes it, and a child keeps the parent's per-thread heap identity. Until a later release makes the heap fork-safe, the child of a process with more than one thread calls only the clone-safe functions of `api/index.md` until it execs or exits, and a child that goes on without exec creates no thread. - **Locks.** The mutex, condition variable, read-write lock, barrier and once of `` are private to one process: in `MAP_SHARED` memory that two processes share, a waiter in one is never woken by the other. A child's copy of a lock is a lock of its own, in the state the fork found it. The child of a raw `clone` system call, which has no lightc thread of its own, may call only the clone-safe functions of `api/index.md`, as [`test_index`](#handlers) checks. ### Exit {#exit} | Default | Enforced by | |---|---| | Returning from `main` calls `exit_group` with its value, which ends every thread of the process. It runs no `lc_atexit` handler. | `regress/RT-main-exit`, `test_lifecycle`: `test_main_return_ends_process` | | `lc_exit` runs the `lc_atexit` handlers once, last registered first, then calls `exit_group`. A second `lc_exit`, from a handler or a signal, ends the process at once, without running them again. `lc_kernel_exit` calls `exit_group` directly. | `test_lifecycle`: `test_exit_reentrancy_guard` | | `lc_lifecycle_enable_shutdown_signals` makes SIGTERM and SIGINT call `lc_exit(0)`. One that comes while the exit handlers run is ignored, so they finish. | `test_lifecycle`: `test_shutdown_signal_runs_handlers` | With the shutdown signals, the exit handlers run in a signal handler, in the middle of whatever the signal interrupted: each then keeps to [what a handler may call](#handlers), as what it frees, closes or flushes may be in use at that moment. A thread ends when its function returns, and only that thread does. Its return value is its exit status, which nothing collects: hand a result back through the thread's argument. ## Async I/O {#rings} - **A ring belongs to one thread at a time.** The `lc_async_*` calls take no lock, so threads that share a ring do so under a lock of their own, as `examples/httpd.c` does. - **A lightio loop belongs to the thread that runs it.** `lio_loop_run_threaded` gives each worker a ring and a coroutine pool of its own. `lio_read`, `lio_write` and `lio_sleep` are called only from a handler's coroutine. - **An entry stays queued until the kernel takes it.** An `io_uring_enter` that fails with `EAGAIN` or `EBUSY` leaves its entries for the next flush or wait, and `lc_async_flush_ex` and `lc_async_wait_ex` report its errno. See [Async & lightio](async.html#async). ## Kernel {#kernel} lightc 0.2.0 needs Linux 5.10 or later, on x86_64 or aarch64. 5.10 is the oldest long-term kernel with every system call and io_uring feature lightc uses: | Needs | From | For | |---|---|---| | `IORING_FEAT_NODROP` | 5.5 | `lc_async_ring_create` fails with `LC_ERR_OPNOTSUPP` without it, as a full completion queue would drop completions | | `IORING_OP_READ`, `IORING_OP_WRITE`, `IORING_OP_SEND`, `IORING_OP_RECV` | 5.6 | the ring's reads, writes, sends and receives, and lightio | | `faccessat2` | 5.8 | `lc_kernel_faccessat2` | `lc_kernel_fchmodat` with flags uses `fchmodat2`, from Linux 6.6.
